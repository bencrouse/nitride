## Plastic Hero / Leads

Make the hook the spectacle: a glossy pointed entrance with a firm simple held
center. Mono/legato,45ms all-pitch glide, small35ms phrase-only common-direction
scoop, quick index/Tone front, tiny global vibrato and short modest Space. No
high network stress, opposed bend, rhythmic gating or evolving ratio routes.
Compare unchanged Copper Lift on the same notes: more immediate pop-hook phrasing,
not a claim to emulate any specific commercial synth or artist's production.

Play a high-register melody, overlap notes for slides, then detach them to hear
the entrance retrigger. The first two notes compare soft/hard touch; the long
held note exposes the simpler body without relying on the attack.

## Budget Heaven / Pads

A bargain-keyboard ensemble played sincerely. Bright triangle-rich near-unison
operator tap, moderate180ms entrance, strong held chords and600ms release, shallow
global vibrato and modest room. The tap's beating is not a new chorus/ensemble
engine. No slow index/filter/ratio evolution or giant ambient tail.
Compare unchanged Soft Alloy: keep the exposed thin/buzzy artificial quality,
not a uniformly warm or tasteful pad. Test actual chord voicings and upper register.

## Sugar Teeth / Physical materials → host Keys & Organs

A tiny abrasive harmonic front with a round restrained key body.1:1shapedFM,
18ms index decay, no filter-FM tine body, no inharmonic bell ratio/pitch entrance/
stress/noise layer. Velocity opens the shared Tone filter; it is last-note velocity,
not independent per-operator or per-voice timbre layering. Small Space and short
release keep the articulation exposed. Compare unchanged Classic Tines: reject
this if it merely sounds like another generic electric piano or mallet.

Use the40/80/120velocity notes, short pop runs, a long gate, low/high notes and
the final four-note chord. Hard/soft contrast and usefulness are listening questions,
not conclusions inferred from the recipe or name.
