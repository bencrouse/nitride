The four revised excitation paths now have different spectral/dynamic identities:

| Character | Voicing inside the resonant excitation branch |
| --- | --- |
| Distortion | Two balanced cubic stages, broad shoulders and a 900 Hz body pole; stronger low odd harmonics without Transistor's edge emphasis |
| Softclip | Smooth tanh knee with lower source sensitivity; rounded, less dense upper harmonics |
| Tape | Arctangent rounding, 2400 Hz branch pole and source-level compression with 12 ms attack / 160 ms release; responds to recent playing within each voice |
| Transistor | Narrow C2 knees, more unequal rails, 1500 Hz pre-emphasis and fast level-relative bias; bright edges and audible even-harmonic/duty asymmetry |

merely making rail heights unequal mostly changes DC in that regime, which the
existing DC blocker removes. Distortion's branch pole emphasizes body instead of
letting its fully clipped signal converge towards the same square-like excitation.
The original source remains present: these are not master output EQ/compressor
effects or hidden loudness compensation.

The established discrete-gradient restoring-force transfers stay separate from
the new excitation voicing. An earlier experiment changing both produced
history-dependent held-note results and was rejected. Every instantaneous
excitation slope remains bounded by one quarter of linear damping; branch memories

accepted Diode bracket/arithmetic is unchanged. Solver precision, 4× processing and

## Integrator-return experiment / 0.7.41 · eval-041

**Eval-039 and eval-040 did not meet the listening goal.** The user again reported
minimal character change and squashing at full Rage. Their passing numerical tests
did not establish useful distortion. The public Pigments description is sufficient
to reject treating all five characters as primarily damping/compression; only Tape
is specifically described as gently compressive. Equations and exact sound remain
independent and unverified against Pigments.

Eval-041 replaces, rather than drives harder, the residual-damping core. Its
state-variable resonator sends the band state through a bounded nonlinear return
**in parallel with linear band-to-low integrator coupling**. The low state returns
to the input, so the distortion participates in resonance before the voice's final
amplitude and summing. It is not a shaper attached after the filter. The linear
coupling remains present at maximum Rage; there is no `shape(gain*band)-gain*band`
subtraction and no hidden output gain or automatic level matching in the plugin.

For integrator states `B, L`, midpoint band `v`, coefficient `g`, and damping `r`:

```
F(v) = v + amount * averageColour(B, 2*v-B)
low  = L + g*F(v)
(1 + g*r)*v + g*g*F(v) = B + g*(input-L)
```

`averageColour` is the divided difference of each character's primitive over the
state step. This discrete-gradient formulation preserves the nonlinear potential
change with coefficients frozen within each sample, rather than injecting energy with naive midpoint
sampling or adding backward-Euler numerical damping. Near equal endpoints,
two-point Gaussian quadrature avoids primitive cancellation. Tape uses mild
attack/release envelope-dependent compression, not a delayed restoring-force
signal that can destabilize the resonator. Diode's asymmetric rational, cubic
Distortion, algebraic Softclip, arctangent Tape and C2 Transistor remain structurally
different transfers. Their slopes may exceed one: the new scalar equation has
positive derivative at least `1+g*r+g*g` instead of relying on the old slope cap.
There are at most 10 safeguarded Newton steps plus 40 early-exiting bisections.

**Behavior changes:** Rage can now colour sound at zero resonance, because the
integrator loop is nonlinear even without extra regeneration. Zero-resonance
neutrality was an implementation assumption, not a documented Arturia requirement
or an automation/state contract. Nonlinear stiffness also moves the resonant peak
with amount and level; cutoff is not an invariant large-signal peak frequency.
Zero Rage retains the exact previous clean-core arithmetic. Classic, parameter
IDs/ranges, preset/session/XML formats and all frozen r1/r2 definitions remain
unchanged. No r3 presets or factory selections are created for this experiment.
R1/r2 loaded in this AU use its revised DSP; archived components/renders are the
only exact old-engine comparisons.

New tests explicitly require retained resonance emphasis and peak energy during a
frequency sweep, in addition to harmonic generation and level-aligned separation.
Tails include low/high cutoff at 48/192/384 kHz. One identical dry source/MIDI probe
is compared across 0/50/100% and against archived eval-040, rather than using changed
presets to demonstrate the effect. Local evidence is append-only under
`evaluation/filters/pass-003/`; no Logic projects/settings are captured.

The comparison command accepts `--baseline-label eval-040 --candidate-label eval-041`
so old/new listening clips and reports identify the actual engines. All audition
examples share active-window RMS matching, not LUFS matching or a listening verdict.
No numerical gate, including the new resonance sweep, establishes perceived quality.

## Stronger Rage / 0.7.40 · eval-040

**Historical, unsuccessful listening checkpoint; superseded by the experiment above.**

The initial eval-039 auditions were too subtle for the user. This revision changes
the Rage DSP, not Classic, host parameter IDs/ranges or state versions. The original
studies, their MIDI/native files, eval-039 component/source and raw/preview audio

### What changed

The old saturators divided their output by drive, making high Rage converge towards
very little feedback and making different characters converge towards one another.
The new regeneration term is `band + amount * (shape(gain * band) - gain * band)`:
the nonlinear residual changes level-dependent damping inside the feedback loop.
It is not a post-filter distortion stage, output boost or an increased uncontrolled
positive feedback gain. Full Rage can reduce fundamental level as it changes tone.
No hidden loudness compensation was added.

Character-specific drive ranges retain asymmetric rational Diode bite, cubic
Distortion, smooth algebraic Softclip, memory/envelope-dependent Tape compression
and biased Transistor plateaus. Transistor uses C2-rounded knees to reduce aliases
from the stronger clipping; it remains an independent model, not a circuit clone.

the original fixed Newton/bisection work bound remains. Higher-drive tests now also
check generated harmonics, gain-aligned amount/character separation and decay to
silence, rather than accepting arbitrarily small numerical differences.

## Original framework / eval-039

## Controls and routing

Open **FILTER…** in Tone. **Classic** is the default and retains the original shared
post-mix 12 dB filter, oscillator/decimator path and shared filter envelope. Existing
sounds are not converted to Rage. **Rage** filters each voice at the existing 4×
internal sample rate, before that voice's amplitude envelope and voice summing.
Space and Output remain downstream. There is no reduced-quality mode or voice cap:
the existing 12-voice limit is unchanged.

Cutoff, resonance, Filter Envelope, key tracking, velocity, cutoff LFO and
Pitch + Tone motion apply to Rage per voice. Filter FM takes each voice's carrier
signal before its filter and amplitude, not a chord mix. The FM routing matrix's
Filter Envelope source is voice-local in Rage and remains shared in Classic.
Global LFOs stay global; they are not per-voice or note-reset oscillators.

Rage amount drives **resonance-feedback distortion**, not a post-filter waveshaper.
In eval-039/040 it had no effect at zero resonance; eval-041 revisits that assumption
as described above. Zero Rage is the clean Rage core,
**not Classic**. Character/amount remain saved while Classic is selected. Family
switching crossfades over 5 ms; character transfers morph over 4 ms. Changing family
on a held note resumes its filter envelope from note/release age rather than
retriggering every note. Normal voice stealing, panic and mono/legato policies remain.

## Independent implementation, not an Arturia clone

The public Pigments 7 manual describes Rage's five feedback characters in §14.3.8,
printed p.166 / PDF p.172:
[manual](https://dl.arturia.net/products/pigments/manual/pigments_Manual_7_0_1_EN.pdf#page=172).
That documents placement and character names, not coefficients or source algorithms.
Pigments is not installed on this development Mac; no sonic equivalence is verified.

Nitride's independently written core uses two TPT low-pass integrators and
nonlinear band-pass resonance feedback. The five transfers differ structurally:

| Character | Feedback model |
| --- | --- |
| Diode | Unequal positive/negative rational knees |
| Distortion | Symmetric cubic knee with a bounded plateau |
| Softclip | Smooth algebraic saturation |
| Tape | Arctangent rounding, feedback low-pass memory and level-dependent compression |
| Transistor | Biased clipping plateaus (C2-rounded knees in eval-040) |

These names describe intended behaviors, not measured analog circuits. Tape is not
a physical hysteresis, wow/flutter or tape-recording model. Each family owns its
topology; this is not a universal Rage core with every future family mapped onto it.
Ripple/all-pass work remains future scope.

The original scalar implicit feedback equation used safeguarded Newton solving (10 iterations,
then at most 24 bisections if needed). Transfer slopes are bounded above by one and the
implicit feedback coefficient is below 0.5. Numerical faults reset only the affected
filter. Each voice has its own core, filter envelope and note modulation state.
Rage uses a separate eighth-order minimum-phase Butterworth decimator; Classic's
original arithmetic stays untouched and warm during family transitions. There is no
block buffering or reported latency change; this is not linear-phase processing.

## Qualification boundaries

`nitride_filter_tests` checks all 106 pre-framework programs against frozen one-second
audio hashes at 48/96 kHz (212 hashes). Core/integration tests cover distinct character
responses, feedback placement, bounded solving, extreme controls, deterministic reset,
independent envelopes, ownership/stealing/release/panic, block partitioning, switching,

`nitride_perf --rage` measures every character at 1/4/12 voices plus an advanced
12-voice workload, at 48/96 kHz and 64/256 frames by default. It must run separately
from builds/tests. `nitride_rage_spectral_tests` reports coherent-tone non-harmonic
energy and 4× versus 32× reference residuals, including cutoff LFO/audio-FM stress.
Non-harmonic energy can also include intrinsic subharmonics/nonperiodic nonlinear
response; the expanded probes report reference non-harmonic energy too. The residual
also includes TPT/decimator response and nonlinear trajectory differences and is
**not** a pure alias measure. Neither these probes nor simple render hashes establish
Pigments fidelity, perceptual quality or an alias-free instrument.

is −86.8 dB or lower for the low-tone probes and −52.6 dB or lower for the high-tone
probes (relative to total output energy). The hardest high Transistor probe is the
worst case here; aliasing is not eliminated. The 32× reference residual ranges
approximately −40.2 to −23.8 dB, with high-tone response/phase differences dominating;

limited core/decimator probes, not full-oscillator alias qualification or audibility
